Before you start — what happens to your voice

DRAFT WORDING — not approved for use with testers yet.

1. What you will do

You will hear three animal calls, one at a time, and record yourself imitating each one, using your own phone or computer. One recording per call — three in all. Each recording is a few seconds. The whole thing takes about ten minutes.

2. Your recording stays on your device

The recording of your voice is made in your browser and never leaves your device. This page has nowhere to send it: it is a plain static page with no server behind it and no upload in its code. When you close the tab, the recording is gone.

3. What does leave, and only if you send it

At the end, the page offers you a small text file to save. You choose whether to send that file back. Nothing is sent automatically. The file contains:

  • a list of numbers describing how the pitch of your voice moved over time, ten times a second;
  • how loud it was over time, on the same clock;
  • how long the recording was, and how much of it had a trackable pitch;
  • the make of your browser and operating system (for example "Chrome on Android") — so a bad result can be told apart from a bad microphone;
  • the tester code from your link, the date, and any of the boxes you tick about how it went.

It does not contain audio, your name, your email, your phone number, your location, or anything you type freely. There is no box in this page to type anything except the tester code.

Plainly, on the pitch line: it is a drawing of how high or low your voice went. It cannot be turned back into sound, it does not contain any words you said, and it is not a voiceprint — the things that make a voice recognisable as yours are not in it.

4. Who sees it

The people building Wild Echo. One further person — an outside judge — will be shown the pitch drawings on their own, with no tester code and no way to know whose they are; their job is to say which animal call each drawing looks like.

5. Where it is kept, and for how long

On the computer of the person running the test. Not published, not posted anywhere, not sold, not given to anyone else, and not used to train any model. It is kept until the decision this test exists for, on 14 August 2026, and deleted within 30 days after that — by 13 September 2026. If that decision date moves, the deletion date moves with it and you will be told.

6. Changing your mind

Reply to whoever sent you this link, quote your tester code, and ask for your file to be deleted. No reason needed, and it will be done within seven days. You can do this any time before the deletion date above. One honest limit: if a total has already been reported — "24 of 30 recordings passed" — your file is still deleted, but that already published total is not recalculated.

7. It is voluntary

You can stop at any point, for any reason, by closing the tab. Nothing is kept and nobody is told. You do not have to send the file at the end even after recording.

8. Who this is for

Adults, 18 or over. Children are not part of this test.

9. About your link

The code in your link is a label, not a lock. It exists so ten people's results can be told apart without using anybody's name. Anyone who has the link can open this page, and the page cannot tell who is holding it. It is said plainly here rather than dressed up as security.

10. Questions

Ask the person who sent you the link. They can reach the people who built this.
